Proof of Patience

A transaction isn't real when you send it. It's real when enough blocks have stacked on top of it that undoing it would cost more than anyone is willing to pay. Finality isn't an event. It's an accumulation.

I think about this more outside of work than in it, because it turns out to be the most honest model of a life I've found. Nothing important confirms instantly. Skill, health, a marriage, a reputation — they all settle the way transactions do: one unremarkable block at a time, each one almost worthless on its own, each one making everything underneath it harder to reverse.

Crypto is a terrible teacher here, which is the joke of it. The industry that coined "low time preference" is the most impatient place on earth. Charts refresh every second. Fortunes appear in a quarter and vanish in a weekend. Spend enough time in it and your nervous system starts to believe that anything worth having arrives in a candle.

It took me years to notice the damage. I was checking a price chart forty times a day and my own life almost never. If my health had a ticker, it was drifting down and I wasn't watching it. If my friendships had a chart, half of them were flatlining from inactivity. I had put all my attention where the feedback was fastest, which is exactly where it mattered least.

The fix wasn't discipline. It was reframing. I started treating the slow things in my life like pending transactions — already sent, already valid, just waiting on confirmations. The workout that changes nothing today. The unglamorous week of writing. The call to a friend that has no visible effect. Blocks. You don't refresh a pending transaction every four seconds and you shouldn't refresh your life that way either. You check that it was sent honestly, and then you go produce the next block.

Impatience, I've come to think, is a kind of reorg attack against yourself — tearing up settled work because a louder chain showed up this week. Every pivot chasing the new narrative, every diet abandoned in week two, every friendship dropped for a busy season is a double-spend of the same hours. The chain with the most accumulated work wins, in consensus and in character.

Send honestly. Wait for confirmations. Keep producing blocks.
