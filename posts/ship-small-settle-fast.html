Ship Small, Settle Fast

My worst months as a builder all have the same shape: one glorious branch, three weeks old, four features deep, that I'm going to ship "once it's ready." It is never ready. It grows until merging it feels like a launch, and launches are scary, so it grows some more. The roadmap becomes a place where work goes to feel safe.

The way out, for me, was stealing the design principle of the thing I build on. A chain doesn't save up transactions for a big quarterly release. It produces blocks: small, regular, final. No block is impressive. The chain is.

So the practice is three rules, and the rules are boring on purpose.

Ship small. If a change can't go out inside a week, it isn't a change, it's a project — and it needs to be cut until a one-week slice falls out of it. Small ships have small blast radii. When something breaks, and it will, you're debugging a haiku instead of a novel.

Settle fast. Shipped means finalized: merged, deployed, announced, done. Not sitting on staging waiting for confidence, which never arrives on its own. The announcement matters more than it seems — a ship nobody heard about is a pending transaction, and pending things are the ones you re-litigate at 2 a.m. Finality is a mercy. It frees the part of your head that was holding the position open.

No empty blocks, no forced blocks. The cadence is the commitment, not the size. Some weeks the block is a real feature; some weeks it's a bug fix and a better error message. That still counts — the chain doesn't apologize for a quiet block. What's not allowed is skipping the block to make next week's "bigger," because that's how the glorious branch is born again.

The compounding is the point. Fifty small ships a year beats four big ones, not because the sum is larger but because each ship returns information, and information early is worth more than polish late. Users tell you which third of the roadmap was imaginary. You find the real product by shipping toward it, block by block.

Small, regular, final. The chain doesn't sprint. It just never stops.
