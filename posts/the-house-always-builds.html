The House Always Builds

Everyone knows the saying: the house always wins. Fewer people ask why. The house doesn't win because it's smarter than the players. It wins because it's still there in the morning. The players come for a night; the house is a building.

Crypto runs on a four-year mood swing, and every cycle produces the same two characters. The trader, who is brilliant for eighteen months and then quietly deletes the app. And the builder, who looks like an idiot for eighteen months — shipping into silence, explaining the product to an empty room — and then suddenly looks like a genius, mostly by not having left.

I've been both. Trading taught me things I use every day: how liquidity actually moves, what people do when they're scared, how fast "community" evaporates when the number goes down. But trading is playing at someone else's table. Your upside is capped by your stack and your nerve, and the game forgets you the moment you stand up.

Building is buying the table. The expected value of any single ship is small and usually negative — most things I've launched died politely. But the edge compounds in a way a position never does. Code survives. Reputation survives. The eleven users who stayed through the drawdown survive, and they tell people. You're not betting on a hand; you're collecting a small rake on every future hand played in a room you built.

The quiet part of the cycle is the whole game. When fees are dust and the timeline has moved on to whatever's next, your competition drops by ninety percent and the cost of attention drops with it. Bear markets are not the punishment for the party. They're the construction season — the only stretch of time long enough to build anything with a foundation.

So this site is the paper trail of that bet. Essays on the craft, the strange economy it lives in, and the life that has to hold it all together. I don't know which projects will make it. I know the posture that will: show up, ship, stay solvent — in money and in spirit — until the players come back.

They always come back. And the house will still be here.
